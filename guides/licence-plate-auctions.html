Licence Plate Auctions Worldwide: Hong Kong, Dubai, UK & Singapore

Four of the world's biggest plate markets — how each works, what sells, and the records that define them.

Updated 2026-10-07 · 8988888 Research Desk

Hong Kong

The Transport Department auctions vehicle registration marks. Ordinary marks sell via e-auction; special and personalised marks (up to 8 characters) go under the paddle. Records include “W” at HK$26M (2021), “R” at HK$25.5M (2023), “28” at HK$18.1M (2016), “18” at HK$16.5M (2008) and “88” at HK$11.4M (2025). Auction proceeds go to the Government Lotteries Fund for charity.

Dubai & the UAE

Dubai's RTA sells plates through auctions often run with Emirates Auction. The Most Noble Numbers charity auction set the world record with “P 7” at AED 55M (2023). Other landmarks: AA 9 (AED 38M, 2021), AA 8 (AED 35M, 2022), D 5 (AED 33M, 2016). Abu Dhabi's plate “1” sold for AED 52.2M in 2008.

United Kingdom

The DVLA sells new registrations online and at timed auctions; a large private dealer market resells cherished plates. Records include 25 O at £518,480 (2014), X 1 at £502,500 (2012) and F 1 at £440,000 including tax (2008). Plates must follow DVLA formats and can't make a car look newer than it is.

Singapore

The LTA runs open bidding windows for numbers 1–9999 in each new series, with a minimum bid of S$1,000.

What wins everywhere

  • Fewer characters
  • Single repeated digits
  • 8s (Chinese markets), 1s and 7s (Gulf)
  • Initials and readable words (UK)

FAQ

What is the most expensive plate ever sold?
Dubai's “P 7”, AED 55 million (about US$15M), at the 2023 Most Noble Numbers charity auction.
Can foreigners bid in Hong Kong auctions?
Bidders must meet the Transport Department's registration conditions; check current rules before bidding.
