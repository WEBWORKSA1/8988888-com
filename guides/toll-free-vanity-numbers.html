Toll-Free & Vanity Numbers: How to Get One Legally (and What the 833 Auction Revealed)

Why you can't buy a 1-800 number, how to get one anyway, and what the FCC's first toll-free auction revealed about demand.

Updated 2026-10-07 · 8988888 Research Desk

The short version

  • Toll-free codes are 800, 833, 844, 855, 866, 877 and 888.
  • Numbers are reserved through a Responsible Organization (RespOrg); the registry is administered by Somos.
  • You can't hoard them, and nobody can sell you one for a fee — that's “brokering”, prohibited by FCC rules.
  • Local numbers can be vanity numbers too, and often cost less.

What the rules say

“No person or entity shall acquire a toll free number for the purpose of selling the toll free number to another entity or to a person for a fee.” — 47 CFR §52.107

RespOrgs also may not warehouse numbers — reserve them without an actual subscriber (47 CFR §52.105). In practice: if a website offers to “sell” you 1-800-SOMETHING outright, walk away. Legitimate providers charge for service, not for the number.

The 833 auction (December 2019)

When the 833 code opened, about 17,000 high-demand numbers were requested by multiple RespOrgs at once. The FCC held its first-ever toll-free auction on 17 December 2019. According to the FCC's public report, 1,659 numbers received bids; 35 bidders won; gross winning bids totalled $6.82 million, but because the auction used a second-price format, winners owed just $285,075. The most-requested number was 833-333-3333, requested by 85 RespOrgs (58%). Its price wasn't published.

How to get a vanity number

  1. Brainstorm words of 7 letters or fewer; test them in the vanity converter.
  2. Check availability across all toll-free prefixes — 888 and 877 versions are often free when 800 isn't.
  3. Reserve through a RespOrg or a phone-system provider that acts as one.
  4. Port it into your phone system and put it on every ad.

FAQ

Can I buy an existing 1-800 number from its owner?
Not for a fee. A number can move when a business is sold or by a lawful transfer between subscribers, but selling the number itself is prohibited brokering.
Is 888 as good as 800?
Callers dial them the same way; 800 is better known, but 888 and 877 vanity versions are far more likely to be available.
