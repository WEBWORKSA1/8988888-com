The Economics of Lucky Numbers: What the Research Shows

Lucky numbers are culture — and measurable economics. A plain-English tour of the studies.

Updated 2026-10-07 · 8988888 Research Desk

Housing: Vancouver

Fortin, Hill & Huang (Economic Inquiry, 2014) studied 116,939 Greater Vancouver home sales from 2000–2005. Houses with addresses ending in 4 sold at a 2.2% discount; those ending in 8 at a 2.5% premium. The effect was concentrated in neighbourhoods with a Chinese population share above 18% — evidence the premium reflects buyer culture, not house quality.

Plates: Hong Kong

Ng, Chong & Du (Journal of Economic Psychology, 2010) analysed Hong Kong plate auctions from 1997–2009. Replacing a 4 with an 8 on a three-digit plate raised the price by 134.8% before 2006 and 97.4% after. Woo et al. (2008) also documented superstition-driven price differences in Hong Kong plates.

Price pointing

Realtors list at $888,888 to attract Chinese buyers, citing the 2.5% premium (UBC, 2016). Retailers time launches and sales to 8/8 and Lunar New Year.

Phone numbers

Auction results — ¥2.33M for 8888-8888 (2003), ¥2.25M for a five-8 Beijing number (2020), AED 7.82M for 050-7777777 (2014) — show the same logic in telecoms.

FAQ

Do lucky numbers really affect prices?
Yes — multiple peer-reviewed studies show premiums for 8 and discounts for 4 in markets with many Chinese-speaking buyers.
How big is the effect?
Around 2–3% on Vancouver homes; roughly doubling on short Hong Kong plates.
