How We Score Numbers — Methodology

Transparent rules for every score our tools show.

Updated 2026-10-07 · 8988888 Research Desk

Luck score (0–100)

Each digit carries a weight from its Chinese homophone: 8 (+8), 6 and 9 (+5), 2 and 3 (+2), 0, 1 and 7 (+1), 5 (−1), 4 (−9). The average weight sets a base around 50. Classic combinations such as 168, 518, 888, 89 and 1314 add points; negative ones such as 14, 74, 94 and 250 subtract. The result is clamped to 1–99.

Rarity score (0–100)

  • Runs: +12 per repeated digit beyond two in the longest run; +30 if every digit is identical; +12 if all but one digit match.
  • Few distinct digits: +5 for each digit of length beyond the number of distinct digits.
  • Mirrors (palindromes, 4+ digits): +10. Ladders (4+ sequential): +8 per step beyond three. Repeating blocks (ABAB, ABCABC): +12.
  • Eights: up to +15 in proportion to the share of 8s. Fours: −10.
  • Length: plates gain up to +60 for one or two characters; numeric domains gain up to +70 for two digits and lose value past six.

Tiers and bands

Rarity maps to Standard (<25), Silver (25–44), Gold (45–64), Platinum (65–84) and Legendary (85+). Each asset type has an indicative price band per tier, anchored to the public records on our Hall of Records and to published research. Bands are deliberately wide: real prices depend on the market, the rules, the buyer pool and timing.

What the score is not

It is not an appraisal, an offer, or financial advice. For a human-reviewed range, request a free valuation.
